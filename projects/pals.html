Community / Case study

Leave a stronger handover.

Taeyang Han · PALS, Sogang University · 2025

I first joined PALS as a mentee. When I later helped lead the programme, I wanted the next team to inherit something they could understand and keep improving.

The work behind a welcome

International student mentoring involves the practical details of finding your way: registration, university procedures, language, and belonging. An organization can be welcoming and still be difficult to run if the way it works lives only in a few people's heads.

My role grew from mentoring into co-presidency in Spring 2025 and presidency in Fall 2025. I worked on the structure behind that support: a written constitution, executive transitions, and clearer coordination between teams.

Look at the handoff

Our executive team included Korean speakers, English speakers, and members still building confidence in either language. Repeated misunderstandings looked like a language problem. To understand what was actually happening, I met individually with the leads of finance, events, content, and human resources.

We examined what each team did, how requests arrived, when work was due, and where coordination broke down. That made a more specific problem visible: the teams lacked a shared format and procedure for handing work to one another.

The change

Make the request clear. Make the handoff explicit. Give the next person enough context to act.

I standardized the request and handoff process, while regular team gatherings helped build the relationships around it. My leadership notes describe coordination that had taken weeks happening within hours after those changes. This was an observed operational improvement, rather than a controlled measurement.

Design for the time people actually have

Some members were also working part-time to cover living costs. When participation fell, conversations revealed limited time and energy. I simplified tasks, clarified what completion meant, and assigned work that fit each member's schedule and capacity. Small, achievable contributions made it easier to participate again.

What I wanted to leave

The constitution and transition documents gave the organization a memory beyond any one executive team. The purpose was to make the next handover less fragile, and to let the next leaders spend more time supporting students.

Leave the organization in a better state than I received it.

This is how engineering and community work connect for me. Before asking someone to try harder, I look at the conditions around them. A clearer structure can make a useful contribution possible.
